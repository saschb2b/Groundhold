The official server

wss://groundhold.saschb2b.com/causeway is the server the game offers first: Join's Official row, a party code typed alone and a play/?join= link all connect there. This is its policy. Anyone can run a server of their own (Host your own); its host sets its rules. This page answers what a player may ask of a service that handles names and connections; it is not legal advice.

Who runs it

Saschb2b, who makes Groundhold, runs it on a private server, beside the server of Cyberworld Endless. It is a hobby server with no guarantees: no company behind it, and no promise that it is up, that it is fast, or that it is still there tomorrow. It restarts at a release, which ends the matches running on it, and it can go down for a fault or for good without notice. Nothing you build depends on it: the game plays alone and on your own network without it.

Moderators

Saschb2b is its moderator. A moderator is whoever is given the server's command line (its container's terminal); anyone given it later is named here first, by their GitHub name, with the date.

The rules

  • Names and signals only. There is no chat and no free text, and the server refuses to start with it on. You choose the name the others see: 1 to 16 letters, digits, spaces, - and _, starting with a letter or a digit. In a match the one thing you send the others is a signal, a ring on the map that says "look here": at most three in five seconds.
  • Parties are by invitation; strangers only when you look for them. A party is joined by its code or its link, which only those you give it have. Its leader can remove a member, who cannot come back with that code. Strangers play with you only when a party's leader looks for them (Find players, Find a rival) and every player accepts the match they are offered; for that match they see your name, your Builder and your signals. There is no list of strangers to browse.
  • What gets a player muted, kicked or banned: a name meant to offend, or to pass for Saschb2b or a moderator; signals sent to flood a match or to pester someone; a modified game (orders the game could not have sent, a state or a result changed on purpose, matches broken on purpose); coming back with a new key after a ban. A moderator picks the least that stops it: a mute (your signals reach nobody; you still play), a kick (out for ten minutes, or up to 7 days), and last a ban (out until lifted).

Blocking, and no reports

With nothing written but a name, there is little to report. To stop playing with someone, leave the party or, as its leader, remove them; a new party gets a new code. There is no block list in the game yet: every party is one you were invited to. The moderator acts only on what they see themselves (a name in the roster of who is connected) and keeps no log of who came under which name.

What is kept, and for how long

The server has no accounts. Your game makes a random key on your device (net.cfg in the game's data folder) and sends the server a token made from it for this server's address, never the key itself. The server turns that token into a public identity with a secret of its own: a number that means nothing on another server and cannot be turned back into your key. It sees your connection's IP address, as every server must, and writes it nowhere; the proxy in front of it is set to keep no access log either.

Removing your data

The server knows you only by your public identity, so it cannot look you up by anything else. Two ways:

  • Yourself: delete net.cfg from the game's data folder (on Windows %APPDATA%\Godot\app_userdata\Groundhold\, on macOS ~/Library/Application Support/Godot/app_userdata/Groundhold/, on Linux ~/.local/share/godot/app_userdata/Groundhold/; in the browser, clear the site data of saschb2b.github.io, which clears every page of that site). Your game makes a new key, so you are a new identity on every server. The old one left nothing on the official server's disk but a ban, a mute or a kick, if it had one, which stays with it; the backups age out within 14 days.
  • On request: open an issue on the project's GitHub page titled "Groundhold server: remove my data", with the name you play under (never your net.cfg). While you are connected the moderator finds your public identity in the roster and runs forget, which takes it out of the players' log (there is none on the official server) and out of any kick. A ban or a mute stays while it lasts. The backups are not edited: they age out within 14 days. While you are not connected the roster does not list you, and there is nothing else to find you by.
