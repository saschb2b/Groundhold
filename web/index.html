Groundhold

Every land was raised out of the Tide, and the Tide wants it back; Builders from every shore hold one another's ground. A co-op tower defense for one to four players: each player is a Builder with three towers of their own, and the party holds one road together, from the Welling to the Holdfast.

The hub

The game opens on the Causeway at low water. The console shows your party, your Builder in the arch with the checks the party covers, the modes and one button. Alone, Play starts a match at once.

Every tower forks once, for good, into one of two branches: one does more of what the tower does, the other covers something its Builder lacks. Tamsin's Skyward Loft and Rime Spire reach the sky; Edda's Kestrel Flock strikes bounce on to two more creeps. The Owl's Eye marks the toughest creep in reach, and every tower hits it 20% harder.

Builders (B) shows each kit with both branches. Set your main and backup there, or Try one in a practice match.

Play together

In the browser the game plays alone, and Try, at once. Parties in the browser are online only: a page served over https cannot reach a server on your home network. The official server goes up with M5, and with it the party's link that opens the game in a friend's browser and joins the party.

On desktop a party plays over your network. Desktop builds and a packaged server to download come with M5; until then run both from the repository. One computer runs the server (python3 server/main.py, Python 3.11 or newer); Join (J) lists it under Nearby, or type the address its banner prints. That makes a party there; the others join with its code.

A link drops: your towers keep firing, and after 5 s the caretaker plays your seat from your Builder's plan. Your game reconnects, catches up under a bar and gets the seat back at the next build window.

Another tab: a hidden tab stops the game and the caretaker keeps your seat; the game catches up when you come back.

The lands fall out of step: with three or more players the odd one out reloads and plays on; with two the match ends and each game saves a report for an issue.

How it is made

Godot 4.7. Every model, icon, console piece and sound was generated for this game from recipes kept in the repository: low-poly towers with painted bricks, shingles and planks, the Builders as small toon figures built in Blender, the bronze console painted in Python, and synthesized sound effects.

The rules run in an integer simulation that every player's game steps the same way from the same turns; a small Python server passes the turns and compares the games' state hashes. Deterministic scenarios check every rule, a scripted party must win High Water and an idle one must lose. Fonts: Noto Serif, under the SIL Open Font License.
